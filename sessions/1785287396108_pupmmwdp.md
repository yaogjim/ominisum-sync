## 正文

US residential proxies are the bane of the internet. They’re the major source of social media manipulation and spam. A residential proxy runs someone else’s traffic through a real home internet connection, so it looks like a normal person on Comcast or AT&amp;T. Almost no one intentionally runs one on behalf of bad actors, they’ve been tricked or hacked. Usually it’s one of a few things:

- A free VPN app reselling your bandwidth.
- A proxy SDK buried in some free game.
- Malware on a computer, router, or smart TV.

LG recently [moved to ban proxy apps from its smart TVs](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/07/lg-to-ban-residential-proxies-from-smart-tv-apps/) after researchers found that over 42% of its apps could covertly turn your TV into a proxy.

#### Why they’re so hard to block

Internet services (including social media apps) can dramatically reduce abuse by blocking entire IP ranges based on country of origin, organization, or type (hosting providers). But a company can’t block US residential IPs if it would also cut off many of their real users. This is why bad actors love US residential proxies, their abuse comes from the same addresses as genuine users.

There’s a whole industry of “threat intelligence” companies that track IPs and sell this information for a lot of money. It’s readily available if you pay for it (and they could just report abuse to internet providers), but there are obviously [perverse incentives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perverse_incentive) here, since significantly reducing the problem would reduce their value.

#### A national security threat

The US government (probably the NSA) should be cracking down hard on US residential proxy networks. They’re a genuine national security threat:

- Actual data and identity loss of American citizens.
- Malware that can record video and audio from infected devices.
- Infrastructure for foreign covert influence campaigns.
- Botnets used in hacking and DoS attacks.

#### ISPs should warn their customers

At the very least, major American ISPs (Comcast, AT&amp;T) should detect clearly suspicious activity coming from customer IPs and warn them to scan their computers, check their TV apps, and find whatever is turning their internet into a proxy. It’s very bad for the customers too, it slows things down and gets their IP banned.

None of this is unique to the US. Every country should be doing the same as part of running a healthy and secure internet of its own.

#### 住宅代理：被忽视的国家安全威胁

##### 什么是住宅代理

住宅代理会借用普通家庭的网络连接转发流量，让恶意行为看起来像来自 Comcast、AT&T 等运营商的真实用户。

大多数用户并不知情，设备通常通过以下方式成为代理节点：

- 免费 VPN 转售用户带宽
- 免费游戏或应用暗藏代理 SDK
- 电脑、路由器、智能电视感染恶意软件

例如，研究发现 LG 智能电视中曾有大量应用可能在后台将设备变成代理节点。

##### 为什么难以封禁

平台可以直接屏蔽数据中心、特定机构或高风险国家的 IP，但无法大规模封禁美国住宅 IP，否则会误伤大量正常用户。

因此，住宅代理能把垃圾信息、账号操纵和网络攻击隐藏在真实用户流量中，成为恶意行为者的理想基础设施。

##### 主要安全风险

住宅代理网络不仅是互联网治理问题，也可能构成国家安全威胁：

- 窃取公民数据与身份信息
- 调用感染设备的摄像头和麦克风
- 支撑境外隐蔽舆论影响活动
- 组建僵尸网络，实施入侵和 DDoS 攻击

##### 产业中的利益矛盾

部分威胁情报公司通过追踪并出售恶意 IP 数据获利。作者认为，它们虽然掌握相关信息，却未必有足够动力推动问题根治，因为威胁减少也可能削弱其商业价值。

##### 应对建议

政府应将住宅代理网络视为关键网络安全问题并加强打击。互联网运营商至少应：

1. 识别用户 IP 发出的异常代理流量  
2. 主动向用户发出安全警告  
3. 提醒用户检查电脑、路由器和智能电视应用  
4. 协助定位并清除恶意软件或代理程序  

这也能保护用户自身，避免网速下降或住宅 IP 被平台封禁。

##### 核心结论

住宅代理利用真实家庭网络掩护恶意活动，兼具隐蔽性、规模化和高破坏性。它不是美国独有的问题，各国政府与运营商都应将其纳入本国互联网安全治理体系。
