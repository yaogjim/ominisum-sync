## 正文

![Chestnut eGPU dock graphic: PCIe Gen4 x4 to USB4, open-source ASM2464PD firmware, $249 dock or $799 with a Radeon RX 9060 8GB](https://hwbusters.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/comma-chestnut-egpu-hero.jpg)

*Built so a car can run a bigger driving model. Interesting to the rest of us because you can finally read the firmware on the bridge chip.*

George Hotz’s comma.ai shipped something on Wednesday that the external-graphics crowd has been asking for since Thunderbolt 3 made eGPUs a thing: a dock whose firmware you can actually read. The comma.ai eGPU dock is called chestnut, it is a PCIe Gen4 x4 to USB4 bridge, and the C source driving its controller is sitting in the open on GitHub under the tinygrad organisation.

There are two versions. **Tiny chestnut is $249** and it is the dock alone, bring your own card. **Chestnut “ready to drive” is $799** and arrives with an AMD Radeon RX 9060 8GB plus the cabling and mounting hardware. Both carry a 30-day money-back trial and a one-year hardware warranty, per [comma’s announcement post](https://blog.comma.ai/chestnut/).

The reason it exists has nothing to do with PC gaming. Comma has spent a decade squeezing openpilot’s driving models into roughly a 10 W on-device power budget, and that ceiling finally became the bottleneck. Chestnut raises it to about 100 W by hanging a real desktop GPU off a comma four. The company reckons the combination lands in the same compute class as Tesla’s HW4.

That headroom is going somewhere specific. Comma is launching a 1B-parameter driving model alongside it in openpilot 0.11.2, which by its own numbers has 30x the parameters and 100x the FLOPs of the latest on-device model, and is the first of what it calls chestnut-class models. The company also says its fleet now logs close to a million driving minutes a day, with 15% of those drives pulled into the training cluster.

#### Why a dock for a car matters to PC builders

Strip away the automotive framing and you have a USB4 dock that comma explicitly says works with an ordinary computer. That is the part worth paying attention to. eGPU enclosures have always been closed boxes: you buy the chassis, you trust whatever the vendor flashed onto the bridge silicon, and when it misbehaves at 40 Gbps your only recourse is a firmware blob and a forum thread.

Chestnut breaks that open. The [repository](https://github.com/tinygrad/asm2464pd-firmware/) holds a C firmware for the ASM2464PD, the ASMedia USB4/Thunderbolt-to-NVMe bridge controller that turns up in a lot of USB4 NVMe enclosures. The README is refreshingly blunt about what works and how quickly: a control message that reads and writes PCIe TLPs manages 3.6 MB/s writing and 1.8 MB/s reading, which is slow, but it can touch anywhere on the PCIe BAR. A separate DMA path into the chip’s 512 KB of SRAM hits around 700 MB/s over 10 Gbps USB3.

Those are not throughput figures for gaming. They are the debug and control plane, and that is exactly the layer nobody has ever been able to poke at on a retail enclosure.

Two caveats worth keeping in view. Gen4 x4 over USB4 is still a narrow pipe next to the x16 slot an RX 9060 was designed for, so this is not a stealth route to full desktop performance. And an open firmware repo is not the same thing as a supported product: comma is selling this to people who want to run bigger driving models, not to Linux users after a better Razer Core. [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Comma.ai-PCIe-Gen4-USB4-Dock) notes it is also compatible with tinygrad workloads, which is unsurprising given who wrote the firmware.

Still, at $249 for a dock you can audit and reflash, it is a more interesting object than the price tag suggests. Mounting your graphics card in the passenger footwell remains optional.

#### Comma.ai 推出开源固件 eGPU 扩展坞 Chestnut

Comma.ai 推出的 **Chestnut** 原本是为了让汽车运行更大的自动驾驶模型，但它对普通 PC 用户同样有吸引力：这是一款采用 **PCIe 4.0 x4 转 USB4** 的 eGPU 扩展坞，其桥接芯片固件完全开源，可供用户查看、修改和重新刷写。

##### 1. 产品版本与售价

Chestnut 提供两个版本：

- **Tiny Chestnut：249 美元**
  - 仅包含扩展坞；
  - 用户需自行准备显卡。
- **Chestnut“ready to drive”：799 美元**
  - 配备 **AMD Radeon RX 9060 8GB**；
  - 包含连接线和安装硬件。

两个版本均提供：

- **30 天退款试用**
- **1 年硬件保修**

其控制器使用的 C 语言固件已发布在 GitHub 的 **tinygrad** 组织下，用户可以审查和重新刷写。

##### 2. 核心用途：提升汽车端模型算力

Chestnut 并非为 PC 游戏而设计，而是为了突破 Comma 自动驾驶硬件的功耗与算力限制。

- 过去十年，Comma 一直尝试在约 **10W** 的车载功耗预算内运行 openpilot 驾驶模型；
- 随着模型增大，10W 已成为主要瓶颈；
- Chestnut 可让 **comma four** 外接桌面级 GPU，将功耗和算力空间提升至约 **100W**；
- Comma 认为，这套组合的计算能力与 **特斯拉 HW4** 属于同一等级。

##### 3. 配套推出更大的自动驾驶模型

Comma 将在 **openpilot 0.11.2** 中推出首个“Chestnut 级”驾驶模型：

- 参数规模达到 **10 亿（1B）**；
- 参数量约为最新车载模型的 **30 倍**；
- 计算量（FLOPs）约为其 **100 倍**。

在训练数据方面：

- Comma 车队每天记录接近 **100 万分钟**的驾驶数据；
- 其中约 **15%** 会被送入训练集群。

##### 4. 为什么它也值得 PC 用户关注

去掉汽车应用背景后，Chestnut 本质上是一款可连接普通计算机的 **USB4 eGPU 扩展坞**。

传统 eGPU 扩展坞通常是“封闭黑盒”：

- 用户无法查看桥接芯片的固件；
- 遇到 40Gbps 连接故障时，往往只能依赖厂商提供的固件文件和论坛经验；
- 很难深入调试底层 PCIe 与 USB4 通信。

Chestnut 的不同之处在于，它开放了桥接层固件，使开发者能够审查、修改和调试这一过去难以触及的部分。

##### 5. 开源固件与实测能力

Chestnut 的固件面向 **ASM2464PD** 控制器。该芯片是 ASMedia 推出的 USB4/Thunderbolt 转 NVMe 桥接控制器，也常见于 USB4 NVMe 硬盘盒。

目前固件展示了两种主要数据路径：

1. **PCIe 控制与调试路径**
   - 可读取、写入 PCIe TLP；
   - 写入速度约 **3.6MB/s**；
   - 读取速度约 **1.8MB/s**；
   - 虽然速度较慢，但可以访问 PCIe BAR 中的任意位置。

2. **DMA 数据路径**
   - 可通过 DMA 访问芯片内置的 **512KB SRAM**；
   - 在 **10Gbps USB 3** 连接下，速度约为 **700MB/s**。

这些数据并不是游戏性能指标，而是底层控制和调试能力的体现；其价值在于，用户终于能够操作普通零售 eGPU 扩展坞中通常封闭的桥接层。

##### 6. 局限与注意事项

Chestnut 仍有两个重要限制：

- **带宽有限**：PCIe 4.0 x4 经 USB4 连接，远不及桌面显卡通常使用的 PCIe x16 插槽，因此无法提供完整的桌面级显卡性能。
- **并非面向普通 PC 用户重点维护**：开源固件不等于成熟、完善的消费级支持。Comma 的主要目标用户是运行大型驾驶模型的人，而不是寻找 Razer Core 替代品的 Linux 或游戏用户。

此外，Chestnut 也兼容 **tinygrad** 工作负载，这与其固件由 tinygrad 团队开发密切相关。

##### 总结

Chestnut 最大的意义不只是其 **249 美元**的裸机价格，而是它提供了一款可以审查、修改和重新刷写固件的 USB4 eGPU 扩展坞。虽然它受限于 PCIe x4 带宽，也并非专门为游戏和普通桌面用户打造，但对自动驾驶、AI 计算、硬件调试及开源固件开发者而言，具有较高的实验和研究价值。
