- **核心高价值点**：把分散在照片、消息、地理位置和交易记录中的碎片化个人数据，通过百科结构与大模型串联成可检索、可追溯的长期记忆系统。
- **留存建议**：中长期保留，适合作为个人知识库和数字归档方向的灵感参考（提供了将个人照片、聊天记录和多源数据整理为可浏览百科的实践案例，具备方法启发性和迁移价值）

**知识要点**

- MediaWiki 的页面、分类、讨论页和修订历史天然适合承载个人知识与事件关系网
- EXIF、位置时间线、银行交易、Uber 记录和 Shazam 历史等多源数据可用于重建事件脉络
- 口述史采访与 AI 自动转录结合，能显著提升家族记忆整理效率
- 大模型擅长在百科体裁下组织叙事，并可跨数据源发现被忽略的关联

**行动项**

- 挑选一个小型个人数据集（如一次旅行照片）试做一页结构化事件记录
- 评估是否用 Wiki、笔记软件或数据库承载个人长期记忆归档
- 梳理可导出的个人数据源清单，并优先处理照片、位置和消费记录
- 如果涉及隐私敏感内容，先设计本地运行、权限控制和数据脱敏方案

## 正文

#### Personal Encyclopedias

Posted by

[Jeremy](https://x.com/jrmyphlmn)

Last year, I visited my grandmother's house for the first time after the pandemic and came across a cupboard full of loose old photos. I counted 1,351 of them spanning all the way from my grandparents in their early 20s, my mom as a baby, to me in middle school, just around the time when we got our first smartphone and all photos since then were backed up online.

Everything was all over the place so I spent some time going through them individually and organizing them into groups. Some of the initial groups were based on the physical attributes of the photograph like similar aspect ratios or film stock. For example, there was a group of black/white 32mm square pictures that were taken around the time when my grandfather was in his mid 20s.

As I got done with grouping all of them, I was able to see flashes of stories in my head, but they were ephemeral and fragile. For instance, there was a group of photos that looked like it was taken during my grandparents' wedding but I didn't know the chronological order they were taken because [EXIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exif) metadata didn't exist around that time.

So I sat down with my grandmother and asked her to reorder the photos and tell me everything she could remember about her wedding. Her face lit up as she narrated the backstory behind the occasion, going from photo to photo, resurfacing details that had been dormant for decades. I wrote everything down, recorded the names of people in some of the photos, some of whom I recognized as younger versions of my uncles and aunts.

After the "interview", I had multiple pages of notes connecting the photos to events that happened 50 years ago. Since the account was historical, as an inside joke I wanted to see if I could clean it up and present it as a page on Wikipedia so I could print it and give it to her. So I cloned [MediaWiki](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/MediaWiki), spun up a local instance, and began my editorial work. I used the [2011 Royal Wedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_of_Prince_William_and_Catherine_Middleton) as reference and drafted a page starting with the classic infobox and the lead paragraph.

I split up the rest of the content into sections and filled them with everything I could verify like dates, names, places, who sat where. I scanned all the photos and spent some time figuring out what to place where. For every photo placement, there was a follow up to include a descriptive caption too.

Whenever I mentioned a person, I linked them to an empty [stub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Stub) page. After I found out I could also link to the real Wikipedia, I was able to link things to real pages that provided wider context to things like venues, rituals, and the political climate around that time, like for instance a [legal amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periyar_and_women%27s_rights#Self-respect_marriages) that was relevant to the wedding ceremony.

In two evenings, I was able to document a full backstory for the photos into a neat article. These two evenings also made me realize just how powerful encyclopedia software is to record and preserve media and knowledge that would've otherwise been lost over time.

This was so much fun that I spent the following months writing pages to account for all the photos that needed to be stitched together.

I got help from [r/genealogy](https://www.reddit.com/r/Genealogy) about how to approach recording oral history and I was given resources to better conduct interviews, shoutout to [u/stemmatis](https://www.reddit.com/user/stemmatis)! I would get on calls with my grandmother and people in the family, ask them a couple of questions, and then write. It was also around this time that I began using [audio transcription](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/speech-to-text) and language models to make the editorial process easier.

Over time, I managed to write a lot of pages connecting people to different life events. The encyclopedia format made it easy to connect dots I would have never found on my own, like discovering that one of the singers at my grandparents' wedding was the same nurse who helped deliver me.

After finding all the stories behind the physical photos, I started to work on digital photos and videos that I had stored on Google Photos. The wonderful thing about digital photos is that they come with EXIF metadata that can reveal extra information like date, time, and sometimes geographical coordinates.

This time, without any interviews, I wanted to see if I could use a language model to create a page based on just browsing through the photos. As my first experiment, I created a folder with 625 photos of a family trip to [Coorg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu_district) back in 2012.

I pointed [Claude Code](https://www.claude.com/product/claude-code) at the directory and asked it to draft a wiki page by browsing through the images. I hinted at using [ImageMagick](https://imagemagick.org) to create contact sheets so it would help with browsing through multiple photos at once.

After a few minutes and a couple of tokens later, it had created a compelling draft with a detailed account of everything we did during the trip by time of day. The model had no location data to work with, just timestamps and visual content, but it was able to identify the places from the photos alone, including ones that I had forgotten by now. It picked up details on the modes of transportation we used to get between places just from what it could see.

After I had clarified who some of the people in the pictures were, it went on to identify them automatically in the captions. Now that I had a detailed outline ready, the page still only had content based on the available data, so to fill in the gaps I shared a list of anecdotes from my point of view and the model inserted them into places where the narrative called for them.

The Coorg trip only had photos to work with. My trip to [Mexico City](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TAchDRv5USw) in 2022 had a lot more. I had taken 291 photos and 343 videos with an iPhone 12 Pro that included geographical coordinates as part of the EXIF metadata.

On top of that, I exported my location timeline from Google Maps, my Uber trips, my bank transactions, and Shazam history. I would ask Claude Code to start with the photos and then gradually give it access to the different data exports.

Here are some of the things it did across multiple runs:

1. It cross-referenced my bank transactions with location data to ascertain the restaurants I went to.
2. Some of the photos and videos showed me in attendance at a soccer match, however, it was unknown which teams were playing. The model looked up my bank transactions and found a Ticketmaster invoice with information about the teams and name of the tournament.
3. It looked up my Uber trips to figure out travel times and exact locations of pickup and drop.
4. It used my Shazam tracks to write about the kinds of songs that were playing at a place, like Cuban songs at a Cuban restaurant.
5. In a follow-up, I mentioned remembering an evening dinner with a guitarist playing in the background. It filtered my media to evening captures, found a frame in a video with the guitarist, uploaded it, and referenced the moment in the page.

The MediaWiki architecture worked well with the edits, since for every new data source it would make amendments like a real Wikipedia contributor would. I leaned heavily on features that already existed. Talk pages to clarify gaps and consolidate research notes, categories to group pages by theme, revision history to track how a page evolved as new data came in. I didn't have to build any of this, it was all just there.

What started as me helping the model fill in gaps from my memory gradually inverted. The model was now surfacing things I had completely forgotten, cross-referencing details across data sources in ways I never would have done manually.

So I started pointing Claude Code at other data exports. My Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp archives held around 100k messages and a couple thousand voice notes exchanged with close friends over a decade.

The model traced the arc of our friendships through the messages, pulled out the life episodes we had talked each other through, and wove them into multiple pages that read like it was written by someone who knew us both. When I shared the pages with my friends, they wanted to read every single one.

This is when I realized I was no longer working on a family history project. What I had been building, page by page, was a personal encyclopedia. A structured, browsable, interconnected account of my life compiled from the data I already had lying around.

I've been working on this as [whoami.wiki](https://whoami.wiki). It uses MediaWiki as its foundation, which turns out to be a great fit because language models already understand Wikipedia conventions deeply from their training data. You bring your data exports, and agents draft the pages for you to review.

A page about your grandmother's wedding works the same way as a page about a royal wedding. A page about your best friend works the same way as a page about a public figure.

Oh and it's genuinely fun! Putting together the encyclopedia felt like the early days of [Facebook timeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Facebook_features#Timeline), browsing through finished pages, following links between people and events, and stumbling on a detail I forgot.

But more than the technology, it's the stories that stayed with me. Writing about my grandmother's life surfaced things I'd never known, her years as a single mother, the decisions she had to make, the resilience it took. She was a stronger woman than I ever realized. Going through my friendships, I found moments of endearment that I had nearly forgotten, the days friends went the extra mile to be good to me. Seeing those moments laid out on a page made me pick up the phone and call a few of them. The encyclopedia didn't just organize my data, it made me pay closer attention to the people in my life.

Today I'm releasing whoami.wiki as an open source project. The encyclopedia is yours, it runs on your machine, your data stays with you, and any model can read it. The project is early and I'm still figuring a lot of it out, but if this sounds interesting, you can get started here and tell me what you think!

Thanks to Vishnu Dut, Sarah Cheon, Andy Law, Vishhvak Srinivasan, and Raghav Rmadya, who read early drafts and gave great suggestions while I tinkered around.

## 通俗精炼（1）

### 2026-03-27 01:36:56

#### 个人百科：用 AI 把人生数据整理成“可浏览的自己”

##### 核心观点
作者从整理家里老照片开始，逐步发现：**百科全书式的知识组织方式，非常适合保存个人记忆、家庭历史和人生轨迹**。再结合 AI 与各类数字数据，可以把零散的照片、聊天记录、位置轨迹、消费记录等，整理成一套结构化的“个人百科”。

##### 事情是怎么开始的
作者在奶奶家发现了上千张散落的老照片。  
一开始只是按照片特征分类，但真正有价值的是照片背后的故事。于是他和奶奶一起回忆照片顺序、人物、时间和事件，并把这些内容记录下来。

后来他突发奇想，想用 **Wikipedia 的形式** 把奶奶婚礼的故事整理成一篇条目。结果发现，这种方式非常适合把人物、事件、时间、地点和照片连接起来，形成清晰完整的叙事。

##### AI 如何放大这件事
作者随后把这个方法扩展到更多家庭照片、数字照片和视频中，并开始借助 AI 自动整理内容。

例如：
- 仅凭照片内容和时间戳，AI 就能生成一次旅行的详细页面
- 结合地理位置、打车记录、银行流水、Shazam 记录等数据，AI 能补全餐厅、行程、活动和背景音乐等细节
- 结合社交媒体和聊天记录，AI 还能梳理人与人之间关系的发展脉络

也就是说，**AI 不只是整理信息，还能帮人重新发现被遗忘的细节和联系**。

##### 为什么“个人百科”有意义
作者意识到，自己做的已经不只是家庭史，而是一种新的个人知识载体：

- 它是结构化的，而不是零散堆积的数据
- 它可浏览、可检索、可关联
- 它能把“照片、对话、地点、事件、人物”串成完整人生故事
- 它让人重新理解亲人、朋友，以及自己的人生经历

技术层面上，它像一个“人生维基”；情感层面上，它让记忆重新变得鲜活，也让人更珍惜身边的人。

##### 项目的最终形态
作者把这个想法做成了开源项目 `whoami.wiki`。  
它基于 MediaWiki 搭建，用户可以把自己的数据导入，让 AI 协助生成和维护个人百科页面。重点是：

- 数据掌握在自己手里
- 可以在本地运行
- 任何模型都可以读取和协助整理
- 用户自己审核、补充和修正内容

##### 一句话总结
这篇文章想表达的是：**未来每个人都可能拥有一部属于自己的“个人百科全书”，而 AI 会成为整理人生记忆、重建个人叙事的重要工具。**

## 结构化（1）

### 2026-03-27 02:11:04

#### 文章主旨

这篇文章主要讲的是：作者从整理家里的老照片出发，逐步把家庭记忆、旅行经历、聊天记录等个人数据整理成一部 “个人百科全书”。他发现，大语言模型配合百科软件，不仅能帮助保存记忆，还能重新发现人与人之间被遗忘的故事和联系。

##### 1. 从一柜旧照片开始：记忆需要被整理和记录

- 作者在疫情后第一次去祖母家，发现柜子里有**1,351 张老照片**，时间跨度从祖父母二十多岁，一直到作者上中学、智能手机刚普及的时期。
- 这些照片原本非常杂乱，作者先按照片的物理特征分组，比如：
  - 相似的长宽比
  - 相同胶片类型
  - 例如一组**黑白 32mm 方形照片**，大致拍摄于祖父二十多岁时
- 整理后，作者能隐约看出一些故事线索，但这些线索 “短暂而脆弱”，因为老照片没有今天常见的 EXIF 元数据，无法直接知道拍摄顺序和背景。

##### 2. 通过采访长辈，把 “照片” 变成 “历史”

- 作者拿着一组看起来像祖父母婚礼的照片，请祖母帮忙重新排序，并讲述每张照片背后的故事。
- 祖母在回忆中补充了大量沉睡多年的细节，包括：
  - 婚礼经过
  - 照片里的人名
  - 某些亲属年轻时的样子
- 采访结束后，作者整理出**好几页笔记**，把这些照片和**50 年前的事件**连接起来。
- 这里的重要启发是：单有照片只能看到画面，有了人的讲述，照片才真正成为 “可被理解的记忆”。

##### 3. 用 Wikipedia 的方式写家族故事

- 作者起初只是想开个玩笑：把祖母婚礼整理成一篇像维基百科那样的条目，然后打印给她。
- 为此他：
  - 克隆了 MediaWiki
  - 在本地搭建实例
  - 参考**2011 年英国皇家婚礼**条目的写法
- 他把内容整理成典型的百科结构：
  - 信息框（infobox）
  - 导语
  - 分章节叙述
  - 可核实的信息，如日期、地点、人物、座位安排等
- 同时还扫描照片，并为每张图写说明文字。
- 当提到人物时，他先链接到空白条目；后来发现还能链接到真实 Wikipedia 页面，于是又把一些背景信息连出去，比如：
  - 场地
  - 仪式
  - 当时的政治背景
  - 与婚礼相关的法律修正案
- 结果是：作者只用了**两个晚上**，就把一组原本可能逐渐失传的家庭照片，整理成一篇完整清晰的 “历史条目”。

##### 4. 从一次尝试，变成长期项目

- 这次经历让作者意识到：百科软件特别适合保存那些原本容易散失的媒体和知识。
- 之后的几个月里，他继续为更多照片写条目，逐渐把分散的家庭记忆 “缝合” 起来。
- 他还去`r/genealogy`寻求建议，学习如何记录口述历史，并得到用户**u/stemmatis**提供的资源。
- 在和祖母及家人通话采访的过程中，他也开始使用：
  - 音频转写
  - 大语言模型
- 这些工具降低了整理和编辑的成本，让 “采访 — 整理 — 写作” 这条链路更高效。

##### 5. 百科结构的价值：能发现原本看不到的联系

- 随着页面越来越多，百科式写法开始展现出独特优势：它能把人物和事件系统性地连接起来。
- 作者举了一个很有意思的例子：
  - 他发现祖父母婚礼上的一位歌手，竟然也是当年帮助接生自己的护士。
- 这种跨时间、跨事件的人物联系，如果只是看照片，很难自己发现；但放在互相链接的百科结构里，就更容易被看见。

##### 6. 从实体照片转向数字照片：AI 开始独立整理故事

- 在整理完实体照片后，作者开始处理自己保存在 Google Photos 里的数字照片和视频。
- 数字照片的优势在于自带 EXIF 元数据，比如：
  - 日期
  - 时间
  - 有时还有地理坐标
- 作者做的第一个实验，是整理**2012 年一次 Coorg 家庭旅行的 625 张照片**。
- 他把文件夹交给 Claude Code，并提示它用 ImageMagick 生成联系表，以便一次查看多张图片。
- 几分钟后，模型就生成了一篇相当完整的旅行条目，能够按一天中的时间顺序描述他们做了什么。
- 值得注意的是：
  - 模型**没有位置数据**
  - 只有时间戳和图像内容
- 即便如此，它仍然能仅凭照片识别出地点，甚至包括作者自己都已经忘记的地方；还从画面中判断出他们使用的交通方式。
- 在作者补充了部分人物身份后，模型还能自动在图片说明中识别人。

##### 7. 多源数据交叉验证：让记忆更完整、更准确

- Coorg 旅行主要依赖照片；而作者**2022 年的墨西哥城之行**数据更丰富：
  - **291 张照片**
  - **343 个视频**
  - 使用的是**iPhone 12 Pro**
  - EXIF 中包含地理坐标
- 此外，作者还导出了更多数据：
  - Google Maps 位置时间线
  - Uber 行程记录
  - 银行交易记录
  - Shazam 听歌历史
- 他让 Claude Code 逐步接入这些数据，模型完成了很多跨源推理，例如：
  - 把银行交易与位置数据交叉比对，判断去过哪些餐厅
  - 从 Ticketmaster 发票中查出一场足球赛的对阵双方和赛事名称
  - 用 Uber 记录确认出发 / 到达时间和上下车地点
  - 用 Shazam 历史描述某些场所播放的音乐类型，比如古巴餐厅里播放的古巴歌曲
- 一个很生动的案例是：
  - 作者后来提到，自己记得有一次晚上吃饭时，背景里有吉他手表演
  - 模型就筛选出傍晚和夜间拍摄的媒体文件，从某个视频帧里找到了那位吉他手，并把这个细节写进页面
- 这说明 AI 不只是 “整理材料”，而是在帮作者做跨数据源的记忆重建。

##### 8. MediaWiki 为什么特别适合做 “个人百科”

- 作者认为 MediaWiki 的架构非常适合这个项目，因为每增加一个数据源，模型都可以像真正的维基编辑者一样不断修订页面。
- 他尤其依赖了这些现成功能：
  - Talk pages：用来记录研究笔记、补充疑点
  - Categories：按主题给页面分类
  - Revision history：记录条目随着新证据出现而如何演变
- 这些都不需要重新开发，MediaWiki 本身已经提供好了。
- 更重要的是，随着条目不断扩充，过程逐渐 “反转” 了：
  - 一开始是作者在帮模型补记忆
  - 后来变成模型主动提醒作者那些他早已忘记的细节

##### 9. 从家庭史扩展到整个人生：真正的 “个人百科全书”

- 后来作者又把 Claude Code 用到更多个人数据上，包括：
  - Facebook
  - Instagram
  - WhatsApp
- 这些档案里一共有大约：
  - **10 万条消息**
  - **几千条语音留言**

- 权限设计

- 面向技术用户的强控制、可定制工具
