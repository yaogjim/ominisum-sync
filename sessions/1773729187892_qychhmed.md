### How I built my wife a personal AI assistant on OpenClaw (and what actually took time)

A setup guide for people building for someone who will never read a setup guide.

I've built six of these for myself. I know the config, the bindings, the model selection. I thought building one for my wife would take an afternoon.

It took a week. But the delay had nothing to do with the tech.

My wife Emily is a sales rep. She spends her days driving between doctor's offices, fitting calls in between school pickups. She has never once asked for a demo but I like tinkering with AI and figuring out fun use-cases (hence my

[cable management task](https://x.com/cathrynlavery/status/2016678136776130909?s=20)).

That gap, between how I build these things and how she actually lives, is what cost me the week. This guide is an attempt to save you that week.

But before we get into config, I want to show you what this actually looks like when it is working. Because the setup is not the point. What happens after is. It's not about setup productivity p\*rn but how useful the agent is once done.

#### What the agent actually does:

The morning brief

7:30am. Before she gets out of bed, a message arrives in her iMessage thread.

> "Good morning. You have a call with \[NAME\] at 9am and a lunch at noon that's about 25 minutes from the office, so leaving by 11:30 should be fine. It's red day so dress \[CHILD\] in red outfit. \[CHILD\] has swim at 4, bag needs to be in the car. One thing to know before the Patel call: you haven't been to that practice in 8 weeks."

The assistant pulls from her calendar, knows kids schedules from conversations she had weeks ago, daycare emails and calendar events, checks the weather, and tracks how long it has been since she visited each practice on her territory. It runs every morning at 7am without being asked. No app to open.

Receipts and the HSA

She buys something at the pharmacy. HSA-eligible. She forwards the email receipt to the assistant without typing a word. I do the same.

> "Got it. Logged $43.17 from CVS on March 4th, HSA eligible. Running total this year: $612. Submitted to Lively for future reimbursement."

This is where ChatGPT stops being a useful comparison. Going from email to submitted expense without work from her. This would also work if she texts a picture of a receipt.

Facebook Marketplace

Six weeks ago, in a voice memo from her car, she mentioned she was looking for a Snoo bassinet secondhand for a group gift. It set up a cron job to check on it once a day until it found something that matched her specs.

Last Tuesday:

> "Found one. $550, good condition, seller in Westlake. Here's the listing."

Package tracking

She mentioned ordering a birthday gift for her niece. The assistant found the confirmation in her email, pulled the tracking number, and has been watching it since.

> "Your package is out for delivery today. Should arrive by 8pm."

She did not ask. She did not forward anything. It found the order confirmation on its own and surfaced the update at the moment it was useful.

Daycare emails to calendar

Our daycare sends multiple emails per week and we don't want to miss signing up for something or missing an event. Or god forbid it's yellow day at school and we don't realize until we arrive there (yes this has happened.)

The assistant reads it, pulls out every date and event, adds them to her calendar, and sends her a three-line summary.

> "This week's school newsletter: Book Fair is March 12-14, bring cash. Spring photos on March 19, picture day outfit reminder added to your calendar. No school March 21 for teacher planning day, already blocked."

#### The thing none of this required

She did not configure any of it. She did not set up automations. She did not learn a new app. She talked into her phone the way she always has, and the assistant remembered what mattered (with help from me on cron jobs and setup).

That is the actual pitch for building this. Not "AI can do tasks." It is "the assistant lives where she already lives, knows what she has already said, and acts without being asked."

ChatGPT cannot do this. It has no persistent memory across sessions, no access to her email, no ability to run proactively in the background. It requires her to show up, explain the context, and ask. She is never going to do that from a parking garage between calls.

Now here is how to build it.

#### Before you start

Two things need to be true before any of the following matters.

OpenClaw running on a Mac. The gateway needs to be up and reachable. Setup guide at

[docs.openclaw.ai](https://docs.openclaw.ai/), also wrote a detailed guide —

[Step-by-Step OpenClaw Setup for People Who Don't Live in the Terminal](https://founder.codes/guides/openclaw-setup)

BlueBubbles installed. This is what connects OpenClaw to iMessage. It runs a local server on your Mac that OpenClaw talks to over HTTP. Download at

[bluebubbles.app](https://bluebubbles.app/), enable the web API, set a password. My claw is setup through a gmail account in bluebubbles so it looks like it's coming from email.

Once those are true, the rest is config.

#### The setup

If you point your claw or claude at this article section they can set this all up for you.

1\. Create the agent config block

Every agent in OpenClaw is defined in your openclaw.json under agents.list:

```text
{
  "id": "your-assistant-id",
  "name": "Your Assistant Name",
  "workspace": "/Users/yourname/OpenClaw/agents/your-assistant-id",
  "model": {
    "primary": "anthropic/claude-sonnet-4-6",
    "fallbacks": ["openai-codex/gpt-5.4"]
  },
  "subagents": { "thinking": "auto" }
}
```

Claude Sonnet 4.6 is fast, handles voice memos natively, and costs a fraction of Opus. The GPT-5.4 fallback means if Anthropic has an outage, the assistant stays up. Create the workspace folder. It is where the agent's memory files live: SOUL.md, USER.md, ONBOARDING.md. I shared a bit more about memory

[here](https://founder.codes/guides/hire-first-ai-employee).

2\. Add the binding

This is what makes the agent respond only to your partner, not to you or anyone else:

```text
{
  "agentId": "your-assistant-id",
  "match": {
    "channel": "bluebubbles",
    "peer": { "kind": "dm", "id": "+15550001234" }
  }
}
```

Put their phone number in E.164 format. When they text, it routes to their agent. When you text from your number, it routes to yours. One gateway, completely separate experiences.

3\. Add them to the BlueBubbles allowlist

```text
{
  "agentId": "your-assistant-id",
  "match": {
    "channel": "bluebubbles",
    "peer": { "kind": "dm", "id": "+15550001234" }
  }
}
```

The allowFrom list is the gatekeeper. Only numbers on this list can reach the gateway at all.

4\. Voice memos — add this to SOUL.md

When your partner sends a voice memo from iMessage, BlueBubbles receives it as an audio attachment. OpenClaw passes it to Claude Sonnet 4.6, which transcribes and processes it natively. No special config required.

The one thing you do need to add to SOUL.md:

Voice memos are the default input. When a voice memo arrives, transcribe and process it exactly as you would a text message. Reply in plain text. Never reference the fact that it was a voice memo.

Emily holds down the microphone button, talks for 30 seconds, sends it. The agent gets a full transcription and responds in plain text. She is in the car most of the day. Voice is faster than typing in a parking garage. This one decision changed the entire shape of how she uses it.

#### What I learned the hard way

The config took an afternoon. The files took a week. Here is why, and what to do about it.

Formatting will kill trust immediately

AI agents default to markdown. In iMessage, that looks like the bot does not know where it is. Asterisks, dashes, headers — all of it renders as raw text on a phone. The first time someone gets a message that looks like formatting code, they stop trusting the assistant. They are not going to debug why it looks weird. They are just going to stop opening it.

Add this to the very first section of SOUL.md, before anything else:

```text
Voice memos are the default input. When a voice memo arrives, transcribe and process it exactly as you would a text message. Reply in plain text. Never reference the fact that it was a voice memo.
```

Early on, when an API call hit a rate limit, the raw error went straight through to Emily. Not a friendly message. The actual system error with the status code. Her response: "Is this thing broken?"

For a technical person, a stack trace is information. For anyone else, it is proof the thing does not work.

```text
Never send error messages, rate limit notices, or tool failures
to the user. Handle them silently. If you can't complete something,
say so in plain English. Not in system-speak.
```

This goes in every SOUL.md I build now, before anything else.

#### The cold-start problem

Most AI assistants fail not because the AI is bad (anymore). They fail because nobody thought about what happens when someone opens it for the first time and sees a blank input. Non-technical people freeze. They type something awkward. The response is generic. They decide the thing does not work.

Write an ONBOARDING.md that sends the first message before they ever say anything:

"Hey \[Name\]! Before I ask you a few questions to get set up, just so you know — if it's easier, send a quick voice memo and I'll pull everything I need from that. Otherwise I'll ask you one thing at a time."

When the AI already knows who you are before the first message, it stops feeling like a tool.

#### The files are the actual work

When I built my own agent, I filled the files in an afternoon. The context was already in my head. Building them for Emily meant I had to articulate how she moves through her life. Not just know it. Put it in writing specific enough that an AI could use it.

The fastest shortcut: let the AI interview you. Paste this into Claude before you open a blank SOUL.md:

```text
I want to build a personal AI assistant for someone close to me using OpenClaw. Help me generate their SOUL.md, USER.md, and ONBOARDING.md.

Interview me first. Ask about: their job and what a typical day actually looks like. How they communicate. What do they reach for their phone to do when stressed? What does a hard day look like? What would feel genuinely useful versus one more thing to manage? What platforms they are on. Whether they are technical.

After the interview, generate all three files ready to copy into the agents workspace.
```

The tech took an afternoon. What took a week was figuring out how someone else moves through their life.

Emily did not want an AI assistant. She wanted to get through her day with an assistant that felt like a real-one, not AI. Those are not the same thing, and building for the second one is harder than it sounds.

The assistant that works for her is not the most capable one. It is the one that lives where she already is, remembers what she has already said, and does not make her think about it.

That is what you are actually building. The config is just the last step.

The best way to start is not to think about what the agent can do. Think about what she is doing right now that she shouldn't have to be doing herself.

Paste this into Claude and answer honestly:

```text
I want to figure out the first three tasks for a personal AI assistant 
I'm building for someone close to me.

Tell me about them: What does their day actually look like? 
What do they handle that nobody else sees? What slips through the cracks?
What would they never think to ask for help with, but would notice 
immediately if it disappeared?

Based on what I tell you, suggest three starting tasks — one reactive 
(they ask, it does), one proactive (it runs without being asked), 
one ambient (it watches and surfaces things). Explain why each one 
for this specific person.
```

  - 二手商品监控：6 周前她在车里发语音说想找二手 Snoo 婴儿床，助手就创建每日检查任务，直到找到符合条件的商品；后来找到一条 550 美元、Westlake 卖家的 listing。

  - 托儿所邮件转日历：助手会读每周学校邮件，提取日期和事项加进日历，并用 3 行话总结，比如 3 月 12-14 日书展、3 月 19 日春季拍照、3 月 21 日教师规划日停课。

- 第三部分：这套系统为什么比普通 ChatGPT 更有用
  - 妻子不需要自己配置，也不需要学自动化，更不需要打开新 App。
  - 她只是像平时一样对着手机说话，助手负责记住重要信息，并在合适的时候自动行动。
  - 作者认为这才是真正卖点：助手“活在她本来就在用的地方”，记得她说过的话，还能主动执行任务。
  - 相比之下，ChatGPT 做不到这些关键能力：没有跨会话持久记忆、不能读她邮箱、不能后台主动运行，必须等她主动打开、解释上下文、下指令。
  - 对一个常在停车场、通话间隙处理事务的人来说，这种交互成本太高，所以她根本不会用。

- 第四部分：实际搭建需要哪些基础条件
  - 前提 1：Mac 上要运行 OpenClaw，网关要可访问。
  - 前提 2：安装 BlueBubbles，它负责把 OpenClaw 接到 iMessage，并在本地运行 HTTP 服务。
  - 完成这两步后，剩下主要是配置。
  - 作者建议：如果把文章中配置部分交给自己的 claw 或 Claude，很多步骤可以直接自动完成。

- 第五部分：基础配置怎么做
  - 第一步是创建 agent 配置块，写进 `openclaw.json`。
  - 关键字段包括：`id`、`name`、`workspace`、主模型和备用模型。
  - 作者推荐主模型用 `anthropic/claude-sonnet-4-6`，因为它速度快、原生支持语音 memo、成本远低于 Opus；备用模型用 `openai-codex/gpt-5.4`，避免 Anthropic 宕机时系统失效。
  - `workspace` 文件夹里会放记忆文件，比如 `SOUL.md`、`USER.md`、`ONBOARDING.md`。
  - 第二步是加绑定规则，让指定手机号发来的 iMessage 只路由到对应助手，号码用 E.164 格式。
  - 第三步是把对方手机号加入 BlueBubbles allowlist，只有白名单里的号码才能接入系统。
  - 第四步是支持语音：BlueBubbles 会把 iMessage 语音当音频附件传进来，Claude Sonnet 4.6 可直接转写并处理，几乎不需要额外配置。
  - 但必须在 `SOUL.md` 里明确写规则：语音 memo 是默认输入方式，收到后要像文本一样处理，用纯文本回复，不要提“这是语音”。

- 第六部分：作者踩过的坑，真正耗时的地方
  - 第一大坑是格式问题。
  - AI 默认喜欢输出 Markdown，但在 iMessage 里，星号、短横线、标题都会变成很“机器人”的原始文本，用户会立刻失去信任。
  - 所以要在 `SOUL.md` 最前面写清楚：只用纯文本，不用 markdown，不提语音来源。
  - 第二大坑是错误信息直出。
  - 作者早期遇到 API 限流时，系统直接把原始错误和状态码发给 Emily，她第一反应是：“这东西坏了吗？”
  - 对技术人员来说，报错是信息；对普通用户来说，报错就是“不能用”。
  - 因此要明确规定：不要把错误、限流、工具失败直接发给用户；如果做不到，就用自然语言解释。
  - 第三大坑是冷启动。
  - 很多 AI 助手第一次打开时只有空白输入框，普通用户会不知道说什么，随手试一句，得到普通回答后就放弃。
  - 所以要准备 `ONBOARDING.md`，让助手先主动发第一条消息，比如：先告诉对方可以直接发语音，然后一次只问一个问题。
  - 这样用户第一次接触时，不会觉得自己在“操作一个工具”，而像是在和一个已经了解自己的人对话。

- 第七部分：真正费时间的是写对“记忆文件”
  - 作者说，技术配置只用了一个下午，但文件内容花了一周。
  - 因为给自己做助手时，很多背景知识本来就在脑子里；给别人做时，必须把“她怎么过一天”“她在压力下怎么用手机”“什么会真正帮到她”写成 AI 能理解和执行的文字。
  - 作者建议一个捷径：先让 Claude 来“采访”你，再生成 `SOUL.md`、`USER.md`、`ONBOARDING.md`。
  - 采访重点包括：对方工作是什么、一天怎么过、沟通习惯、压力大时最常拿手机干什么、什么帮助是“真有用”而不是“增加管理负担”、是否懂技术、平时用哪些平台。
  - 文中还给出了模板，核心原则包括：
    - 语气要温暖但高效；
    - 默认短回复；
    - 只用纯文本；
    - 只接受指定号码的指令；
    - 设提醒后要确认；
    - 不清楚时只问一个关键问题，不要连问五个；
    - 帮助范围包括排程、提醒、快速研究、代写信息、追踪包裹收据、从邮件提取日期写入日历、监控搜索结果等。

- 第八部分：文章最后的结论
  - 作者最后强调：最耗时的不是技术，而是搞清楚“另一个人是怎么生活的”。
  - Emily 并不想要一个“AI 助手”，她想要的是一个真正减轻日常负担的助手，而且最好让她感觉不到 AI 的存在。
  - 所以最有效的助手，不一定是能力最强的，而是那个：
    - 出现在她本来就在用的地方；
    - 记住她以前说过的话；
    - 不需要她多想，就能主动做事。
  - 作者建议，开始时不要先想“AI 能做什么”，而要先想“她现在有哪些事本来就不该自己做”。
  - 并建议先确定 3 类起步任务：
    - 一个响应式任务：她说了，助手就做；
    - 一个主动式任务：不用她说，系统自动运行；
    - 一个环境式任务：后台持续观察，有重要信息时再提醒她。
