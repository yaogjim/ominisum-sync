## 分析要点

- **核心高价值点**：产品能否成功不仅取决于开发质量，也取决于能否在长期迭代后及时承接平台能力变化带来的新市场。
- **留存建议**：作为独立软件创业的真实个案选择性保留（包含产品收入、维护成本、平台变化和失败周期等具体的一手创业经历，具有案例参考价值。）

**知识要点**

- 个人使用需求可以形成产品起点，但不能替代市场规模和持续维护成本验证。
- 失败产品即使仍有使用价值，收入不足以覆盖系统适配和客户需求时也应停止维护。
- 已有产品、忠实用户和持续迭代能够提升开发者承接平台新机会的能力。

**行动项**

- 评估现有产品收入能否覆盖系统更新与客户需求带来的持续维护成本。
- 持续整理客户反馈并迭代核心产品，为平台能力变化预留快速适配空间。
- 复盘产品收入集中度，识别单一成功产品带来的经营风险。

##### August 10 2026

Ten years and one day ago, on August 9, 2016, I abruptly quit my job. I mark this occasion as the beginning of my indie software developer career. Happy anniversary to me and to my customers!

I didn’t formulate a plan until after I quit. I decided that I could afford to indulge a dream, express myself, design and build my own app from scratch. I could be my own boss for once. My journey as an indie developer mirrored an enduring aphorism: it takes five years to become an overnight success. That night was September 19, 2021. At the time, I was about a month away from going broke, having burned through all of the savings I had accumulated before I quit my job. On the morning of September 20, however, Apple released iOS version 15, which added web browser extensions for mobile Safari. This deus ex machina, a miraculous software update, changed my career, and my life. Suddenly I was running a viable business. Since then I’ve rebuilt my savings to a level higher than before. Don’t worry, though, I’m not retirement planning. On the contrary, I’m a weirdo workaholic who gets bored without a purpose.

My inclination is to refrain from giving business advice, because it would consist largely of survivorship bias. I got lucky, eventually. For five years, I struggled. I failed financially. It was demoralizing, depressing. I felt hopeless. The only thing that kept me in business was my inability to find a new job. It turns out that the industry is not especially interested in hiring a long-experienced software engineer, at least not if that software engineer is middled-aged, lacks a computer science degree (my two philosophy degrees are apparently unimpressive), and possesses outmoded skills such as native Mac app development with AppKit and Objective-C. I had a number of job interviews but no offers. The last time I received an offer of full-time permanent employment was in 2008. Perhaps I wouldn’t have quit that job if I had known it would be my last. Oh well, ignorance is bliss! So who am I to advise others on their careers?

My first app as an indie developer was [Underpass](https://underpassapp.com/Underpass.html), the eponym of my business website [underpassapp.com](https://underpassapp.com/). Underpass provided peer-to-peer, end-to-end encrypted chat and file transfer. You might ask, reasonably, what business a lone developer had building a chat app. In retrospect, I had no business at all. Nonetheless, my inspiration was a [personal use case](https://underpassapp.com/news/Why-Underpass.html), transferring sensitive data between my own devices. I released Underpass for Mac in January 2017 and Underpass for iOS in April 2017. Sadly, Underpass was a flop, its lifetime revenue under $3000. I finally removed Underpass from the App Store in 2021, because Apple kept breaking features in OS updates and customers kept requesting features that I was never going to implement. The minuscule App Store revenue was insufficient to justify ongoing maintenance of the app. Underpass still mostly works, though, and to this day I still use Underpass myself for its original use case, transferring data and files between my own devices. (I’m not a fan of cloud sync and refuse to sign my iPhone into iCloud.)

You might also reasonably ask why a [vehement critic of the App Store](https://lapcatsoftware.com/articles/2026/1/9.html) decided to distribute software exclusively from the App Store. I had two main reasons, one fairly obvious, the other I was reluctant to share but feel comfortable admitting now. The obvious reason is that an iOS version of Underpass was essential to my vision for the app, and the App Store was the only way to distribute software on iOS, unfortunately. The other reason is that I had no confidence my software business would succeed. I figured that I would give indie development a try, make my own app, and if my business happened to fail, I could put my App Store app on my résumé for potential employers to see when I applied for jobs. (Of course I’ve already noted how this strategy was fruitless.) The prospect of low sales and wasted effort deterred me from developing my own independent store outside the Mac App Store. Such an investment can pay off for longtime indie Mac developers, but I was far from certain that I would become one.

My second app was [StopTheMadness](https://underpassapp.com/StopTheMadness/), a Safari extension. I don’t think I’ve ever mentioned this anecdote, but I was inspired to create the app by an offhand tweet from [Tom Harrington](https://www.atomicbird.com/) (no longer on Twitter) complaining about some website annoyance. Thank you, Tom! I developed StopTheMadness very quickly, and after its release in April 2018, StopTheMadness became somewhat popular very quickly, popular enough to be promising. The problem was, StopTheMadness did not achieve enough popularity to pay for all of my living expenses, so I was still depleting my savings. Unbeknownst to me, the next three years of StopTheMadness, the difficult years, were setting the stage for my moment in the spotlight. I continued to improve the app, added many features, responded to customer feedback, and earned a loyal fan base who promoted StopTheMadness via word of mouth. When iOS 15 finally arrived with Safari extension support, I was well placed to seize the opportunity.

StopTheMadness, including StopTheMadness Mobile and StopTheMadness Pro, accounts for more than 93% of my lifetime App Store proceeds. I’ve released a number of [other apps](https://underpassapp.com/) since 2018, trying to expand my product line, but none have reproduced the success of StopTheMadness. I’m basically a one-hit wonder. As long as people love my one hit, I’m happy to keep playing it for them.

What about my next decade? I have no clue! Although I constantly consider ideas, I rarely make long-term plans, and when I do they usually don’t work out as I planned. My most successful plan financially was the major update StopTheMadness Pro, a paid upgrade released in December 2023, but even that was less than a year of work if I recall correctly. My approach to business is to ride the waves like a surfer and hope that I can stay on board. Surfing is not a sport: it’s a way of life.

## 结构化（1）

### 2026-08-17 05:17:46

#### 文章精炼总结

作者回顾了自己从 **2016 年辞职到独立开发满十年**的经历：前五年长期亏损、几近破产，最终因 **iOS 15 支持 Safari 扩展**而迎来转机。其成功既来自持续打磨产品和积累用户，也高度依赖平台变化带来的偶然机会，并非一套可轻易复制的创业方法。

##### 1. 毫无计划地开启独立开发生涯

- **2016 年 8 月 9 日**，作者突然辞职，并将这一天视为独立软件开发生涯的起点。
- 辞职时并没有明确计划，只是想实现一个梦想：从零设计自己的应用，表达个人理念，并第一次真正成为自己的老板。
- 他的经历印证了“**一夜成名需要五年**”这句话：直到 **2021 年 9 月 19 日**，事业才真正迎来转折。

##### 2. iOS 15 带来命运转机

- 到 2021 年 9 月，作者已经花光大部分积蓄，距离破产大约只剩 **一个月**。
- **2021 年 9 月 20 日**，苹果发布 iOS 15，为移动版 Safari 增加浏览器扩展支持。
- 这次系统更新让他的 Safari 扩展产品获得巨大机会，个人事业由此变成可持续的生意。
- 此后，他不仅恢复了积蓄，资产水平还超过辞职前。不过他没有退休打算，因为自己是一个“没有目标就会无聊”的工作狂。

##### 3. 成功背后有很强的幸存者偏差

- 作者不愿轻易提供商业建议，因为他的成功很大程度上源于幸运。
- 在转机到来前，他经历了 **五年的财务失败**，长期感到沮丧、压抑和绝望。
- 支撑他继续创业的一个现实原因，是他始终找不到新工作：
  - 年龄已到中年；
  - 没有计算机科学学位，只有两个哲学学位；
  - 擅长的 AppKit、Objective-C 等原生 Mac 开发技能被行业视为过时。
- 他参加过多次面试却没有拿到录用，最后一次获得长期全职工作邀请还是在 **2008 年**。因此，他认为自己没有资格把个人经历包装成通用职业建议。

##### 4. 第一款产品 Underpass：技术可用，商业失败

- 作者的第一款独立应用是 **Underpass**，提供：
  - 点对点通信；
  - 端到端加密聊天；
  - 文件传输。
- 产品源于个人需求：在自己的设备之间安全传输敏感数据。
- 发布时间：
  - Mac 版：**2017 年 1 月**；
  - iOS 版：**2017 年 4 月**。
- 商业结果并不理想，Underpass 整个生命周期收入 **不足 3,000 美元**。
- 2021 年，作者将其从 App Store 下架，原因包括：
  - 苹果系统更新不断破坏应用功能；
  - 用户持续要求他无意实现的新功能；
  - 微薄收入无法覆盖长期维护成本。
- 虽然产品失败，作者至今仍用它在个人设备之间传输数据和文件，因为他不喜欢云同步，也拒绝让 iPhone 登录 iCloud。

##### 5. 明知批评 App Store，为何仍选择独家上架

作者选择只通过 App Store 分发软件，主要有两个原因：

1. **现实限制**  
   Underpass 的产品构想必须包含 iOS 版本，而当时 App Store 是 iOS 软件唯一的分发渠道。

2. **为求职留后路**  
   作者并不相信自己的独立业务一定会成功，希望即使创业失败，也能把 App Store 应用写进简历，向潜在雇主展示能力。但事实证明，这个求职策略并未奏效。

此外，由于担心销量低、投入浪费，他没有为 Mac 应用自建独立商店。自建渠道可能适合成熟的独立开发者，但当时的他并不确定自己能否坚持到那一步。

##### 6. StopTheMadness：快速起步，长期积累

- 第二款应用 **StopTheMadness** 是一款 Safari 扩展，灵感来自开发者 Tom Harrington 在 Twitter 上随口抱怨的某个网站问题。
- 作者迅速完成开发，并于 **2018 年 4 月**发布。
- 产品很快获得一定欢迎，显示出商业潜力，但早期收入仍不足以覆盖全部生活开支，作者只能继续消耗积蓄。
- 接下来的三年虽然艰难，却为后来的爆发打下基础：
  - 持续改进产品；
  - 增加大量功能；
  - 积极回应用户反馈；
  - 培养忠实用户；
  - 依靠口碑传播扩大影响。
- 因此，当 iOS 15 支持 Safari 扩展时，StopTheMadness 已具备成熟产品和用户基础，能够立刻抓住机会。

##### 7. 一款产品撑起整个业务

- StopTheMadness 系列包括：
  - StopTheMadness；
  - StopTheMadness Mobile；
  - StopTheMadness Pro。
- 该系列贡献了作者 App Store 历史总收入的 **93% 以上**。
- 2018 年后，他还发布过多款应用，希望扩大产品线，但没有任何一款复制 StopTheMadness 的成功。
- 作者称自己是“**一击成名的歌手**”：只要用户仍然喜欢这款产品，他就愿意继续维护和改进它。

##### 8. 对未来：不做长期预测，顺势而为

- 作者对下一个十年没有明确规划。虽然经常思考新点子，但很少制定长期计划，即使制定了，结果通常也不会按预期发展。
- 他财务上最成功的一次主动规划，是在 **2023 年 12 月**推出付费升级版 **StopTheMadness Pro**；但这项计划本身也只用了不到一年。
- 他的经营哲学不是精确预测未来，而是像冲浪者一样：
  - 观察环境变化；
  - 抓住突然出现的机会；
  - 尽力留在浪潮之上。
- 对他而言，“冲浪”不只是经营策略，更是一种接受不确定性、持续前进的生活方式。

## 通俗精炼（1）

#### 独立开发十年：五年积累，等来一次平台红利

作者 Jeff Johnson 回顾十年独立开发经历：他并非靠周密规划成功，而是在长期亏损、求职无果、几乎破产之际，凭借已有产品和用户积累，抓住苹果开放移动端 Safari 扩展的机会，才建立起可持续的生意。他强调，这更像幸运与坚持共同作用，而非可复制的方法论。

##### 第一款产品失败：有真实需求，不等于有市场

2016 年辞职后，Johnson 开发了端到端加密聊天与文件传输应用 Underpass，源于自己在设备间传递敏感数据的需求。但单人开发者很难做好聊天产品，其终身收入不足 3000 美元。由于系统更新不断破坏功能、用户需求超出维护能力，他于 2021 年将其下架。

##### 三年打磨，为突如其来的机会做好准备

第二款产品 StopTheMadness 是解决网页使用烦恼的 Safari 扩展，2018 年上线后虽有口碑，却不足以覆盖生活成本。接下来三年，他持续增加功能、响应反馈并积累忠实用户。2021 年 iOS 15 支持移动 Safari 扩展时，产品得以迅速承接新需求；当时他的储蓄已接近耗尽，只够再支撑约一个月。

##### 成功高度集中，也难以复制

StopTheMadness 系列贡献了作者 App Store 历史收入的 93% 以上，其他产品均未复制其成功，业务实质上依赖这一款“爆品”。2023 年推出的付费升级 Pro 是少数成功的主动商业规划。

这段经历说明：平台能力变化可能突然重塑小型软件的市场，但前提是产品、口碑和持续维护已经到位。作者拒绝把个人经历包装成创业建议，因为其中包含明显的幸存者偏差；他的策略更像“顺浪而行”，而不是长期计划驱动。
