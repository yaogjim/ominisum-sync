### How to Invest in AI Without Just Buying NVIDIA

NVIDIA makes chips. TSMC manufactures them. Amazon rents them. OpenAI trains models on them. Salesforce builds software on top of those models. These are not competitors — they are sequential links in the same chain, and understanding that chain is the starting point for any serious AI investment thesis. Investors wanting exposure to the AI trade often don't know where to start. This article deep dives into the end-to-end AI supply chain and the key companies powering each layer.

The AI stack is a multi-layered ecosystem where each level depends on the one below it. There are broadly six layers: the Hardware Foundation, Infrastructure &amp; Physical Environment, the Cloud &amp; Model Layer, the Data &amp; Infrastructure Software Layer, and the Application &amp; Software Layer. Running alongside all of these is an often-overlooked layer — Raw Materials &amp; Commodities — which underpins the entire physical buildout.

The Complete AI Stack

#### Raw Materials &amp; Commodities

Rarely discussed in AI investing circles, this layer underpins the entire physical stack. Every data centre, every chip, and every power cable requires physical materials. AI's infrastructure buildout is driving structural demand across several commodity markets.

- Copper: The most direct AI commodity play. Copper is essential for power cabling into and within data centres, PCB boards inside servers, liquid cooling systems, and the broader electrical grid expansion needed to support AI energy demand. AI, electrification, and EVs are simultaneously driving a structural copper supercycle. (ERO, FCX, SCCO, COPX ETF)
- Uranium &amp; Nuclear Fuel: As hyperscalers sign long-term nuclear power purchase agreements to secure clean baseload energy, uranium demand is increasingly tied to AI's power needs. (CCJ, UEC, URA ETF)
- Rare Earth &amp; Specialty Metals: Rare earth elements are used in magnets for cooling fans and electric motors, while specialty metals like tantalum and tungsten appear throughout chip manufacturing and server assembly. (MP, Lynas — Private)
- Natural Gas &amp; Energy Infrastructure: In the near term, natural gas remains a significant power source for data centres while nuclear and renewables scale. Midstream infrastructure and LNG exporters benefit from rising energy demand. (LNG, ET, WMB, XLE ETF)

#### The Hardware Foundation

This is the physical layer. Without these components, AI models cannot be trained or run (inferenced). Every layer above ultimately rests on the hardware built here, making it the most capital-intensive and strategically critical part of the stack.

- Equipment: Specialised machines used to manufacture and inspect chips at atomic precision. A single EUV lithography machine from ASML costs over $350 million. (ASML, AMAT, KLAC, CAMT)
- Semiconductor IP &amp; EDA Tools: No chip gets designed without electronic design automation software and licensed chip architecture IP. These companies sit quietly behind every GPU and ASIC in existence. (ARM, SNPS, CDNS)
- Fabrication: The factories/fabs that physically manufacture the chips. Extreme capital intensity (a leading-edge fab costs $20B+) means only a handful of players exist globally. (TSMC, Samsung)
- Design (Custom Silicon): Bespoke chips (ASICs) designed for specific cloud workloads, offering better performance-per-watt than general purpose GPUs for targeted tasks. (AVGO, MRVL)
- Memory (HBM): High Bandwidth Memory sits alongside the GPU and feeds it data at extraordinary speed. It is the short-term working memory of an AI chip. Without enough of it, the GPU starves. (MU, SK Hynix, Samsung)
- Advanced Packaging: Where Memory (HBM) is physically stacked on top of the compute chip using technologies like CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate), dramatically increasing bandwidth while reducing latency. (TSMC)
- Assembly (ODM): ODMs build the boards and racks. They mount TSMC-fabricated chips onto massive motherboards (such as NVIDIA's HGX board), integrating power regulators, high-speed connectors, and cooling systems into a deployable server. (SMCI, Foxconn, Quanta)
- Compute System (GPU/TPU): The full-stack architect of AI. It integrates high-performance logic (GPUs), high-speed memory (HBM), and proprietary interconnects (NVLink) into a unified hardware-software environment. Its primary role is to abstract complex hardware physics into a programmable platform (CUDA), allowing developers to train and deploy massive AI models at scale with turnkey efficiency. NVIDIA currently dominates this layer with \~80%+ market share in AI training chips. (NVDA, GOOGL, AMD)
- Networking: High-speed cables and switches that connect thousands of GPUs into a single supercomputer. Without fast networking, GPUs cannot coordinate efficiently and performance degrades dramatically. (ANET, MRVL)
- Edge AI &amp; Custom Silicon (Vertical Integration): A distinct category where a company designs its own chips exclusively for internal use, running AI inference directly on-device rather than in the cloud. Apple's Neural Engine inside its M-series and A-series chips is the leading example — delivering state-of-the-art performance-per-watt without sending data to a server. Apple does not sell these chips externally, but its 2B+ active devices make it the world's largest edge AI deployment platform. (AAPL)

#### Infrastructure &amp; Physical Environment

AI chips generate immense heat and consume extraordinary amounts of power. A single AI data centre can draw as much electricity as a small city. This layer provides the physical environment that keeps the hardware running.

- Power &amp; Cooling: Modern AI server racks require liquid cooling rather than traditional air cooling, as heat density has exceeded what fans can handle. Power infrastructure must also be purpose-built for AI's continuous, high-draw workloads. (VRT, ETN, MOD)
- Data Centre Real Estate: The physical buildings that house the servers. Hyperscalers and enterprises lease space from specialist operators who manage power, connectivity, and physical security at scale. (EQIX, DLR, AMT, IRM)
- Energy Generation: AI's power demands are so significant that hyperscalers are increasingly signing direct deals with nuclear and renewable energy providers to secure long-term, carbon-aware supply. This structural demand tailwind is a multi-decade story. (CEG, NEE)
- GPU Cloud Operators (Neoclouds): An emerging sub-category sitting between infrastructure and cloud. These companies own and operate their own GPU-dense data centres, often powered by renewable energy, and rent raw compute capacity directly to AI labs and enterprises. They exist because hyperscalers couldn't build GPU capacity fast enough to meet demand. IREN (formerly Iris Energy) transitioned from Bitcoin mining and now operates renewable-powered AI compute facilities. CoreWeave built one of the largest independent GPU clusters globally, counting OpenAI and Microsoft among its customers. (IREN, CRWV)

#### The Cloud &amp; Model Layer

This is where raw hardware is turned into accessible intelligence. Hyperscalers aggregate enormous GPU clusters and expose them to the world via APIs, while AI labs use that compute to train the foundational models that power most AI products today.

- Cloud Hyperscalers: They purchase GPUs at scale and rent access to developers and enterprises. The cloud layer commoditises hardware. Customers don't need to own servers, they simply pay for compute time. When you buy compute from AWS or Azure, you're getting a complete ecosystem — databases, networking tools, security, storage, developer tools, AI APIs, compliance frameworks, identity management, and hundreds of other services. The GPU is just one small part of a massive integrated platform. Their customers are enterprises building entire businesses on top of their cloud, not just running training jobs. They also own their own fibre networks, custom chips (Google's TPU, AWS's Trainium), and have hundreds of thousands of enterprise contracts with deeply embedded switching costs. Oracle (OCI) has emerged as a surprise challenger, signing massive GPU cloud deals with xAI and others by moving faster and cheaper than the traditional three. (MSFT/Azure, AMZN/AWS, GOOGL/Google Cloud, ORCL/OCI)
- Foundational Models: The companies building Large Language Models (LLMs) — the core intelligence underpinning most AI applications. These models require billions of dollars of compute and data to train, creating significant barriers to entry. DeepSeek's emergence in early 2025 showed that efficient training methods can compress costs dramatically, reshaping competitive dynamics. (OpenAI — Private/MSFT partner, Anthropic — Private/AMZN partner, META/Llama, xAI/Grok, Mistral, DeepSeek)

#### Data &amp; Infrastructure Software

Often overlooked, this layer is the data plumbing of AI. A model is only as good as the data pipeline behind it. At enterprise scale, getting clean, structured, real-time data into an AI system is a significant engineering and operational challenge.

- Data Storage &amp; Retrieval: Persistent storage of the structured and unstructured data that AI models train on and query. This includes traditional databases as well as newer vector databases purpose-built for storing AI embeddings. (MDB, ORCL)
- Data Pipelines &amp; Streaming: Tools that move, clean, and transform data in real time from source systems into where AI models can consume it. Without reliable pipelines, models receive stale or incomplete data. (CFLT, INFA)
- Infrastructure Orchestration: Software that provisions and manages the cloud infrastructure AI workloads run on, ensuring reliability, security, and cost efficiency at scale. (HashiCorp/IBM, DDOG)

#### The Application &amp; Software Layer

This is the top of the chain — the software that businesses and consumers actually use. Value here is captured through distribution, proprietary data, and workflow integration rather than raw compute.

- AI Data &amp; Analytics Infrastructure: Tools that help companies build, deploy, and manage their own AI applications on top of foundational models. These platforms abstract model complexity and add governance, security, and auditability for enterprise use. (PLTR, Databricks — Private, AI, SNOW)
- AI-Augmented SaaS: Established software businesses embedding AI copilots directly into the workflows their customers already use daily — from CRM to document creation to IT automation. Distribution and existing data moats are the competitive advantage here. (CRM, ADBE, NOW, PATH)
- Consumer &amp; Ecosystem AI: Apple sits here too, as its Apple Intelligence suite (launched in 2024) brings on-device AI directly to over 2 billion devices worldwide. Its differentiated strategy of running AI locally (rather than in the cloud) gives it a privacy and latency advantage that no cloud-native competitor can easily replicate. (AAPL, MSFT/Copilot)
- Physical AI (Robotics): AI moving from software into the physical world. This includes surgical robots, industrial automation, and humanoid robots — the latter being an early-stage but potentially enormous category. (ISRG, TER, TSLA/Optimus, Figure AI, 1X Technologies, Boston Dynamics)

The single most important insight from mapping this supply chain is that AI is not one investment. It is six different bets, each with different risk profiles, different time horizons, and different competitive dynamics.

The hardware and raw materials layers are largely a picks-and-shovels play. They benefit regardless of which AI model wins, which application succeeds, or which hyperscaler dominates. TSMC makes money whether NVIDIA or AMD wins. Copper gets used whether it's Microsoft or Oracle building the data centre. These are the most durable, least binary positions in the stack.

The model and application layers are the opposite — high upside, high volatility, and deeply winner-take-most. OpenAI could be worth a trillion dollars or be disrupted by the next DeepSeek. Palantir could define enterprise AI or get squeezed between hyperscalers building downward and open-source models building upward.

The vertically integrated players — Google, NVIDIA, Microsoft, Apple, Amazon — sit in the most defensible position of all. They capture margin at multiple layers simultaneously, and each layer they control makes the others harder to displace. This is not a coincidence. It is the playbook.

Knowing where each company sits in this stack doesn't tell you what to buy. But it tells you what you're actually betting on when you do.

This supply chain as it exists today is still early. Several layers are nascent, several are about to be disrupted, and one or two don't fully exist yet. The stack will look different in five years. But understanding it today is the starting point for navigating what comes next.

##### 1. 为什么不能只买 NVIDIA：先理解 AI 全产业链
- 文中用一句话串起 AI 链条：**NVIDIA 造芯片设计，TSMC 负责制造，亚马逊提供算力租赁，OpenAI 用这些算力训练模型，Salesforce 再把模型做成软件产品。**
- 重点不是谁和谁竞争，而是：**它们是同一条 AI 价值链上的不同环节。**
- 对投资者来说，真正重要的是理解：**AI 收益会分散到多个层级，而不是只集中在一家龙头公司。**

---

##### 2. AI 产业大致分为六层
文章认为，AI 生态是一个多层结构，主要包括：
- **硬件基础层**
- **基础设施与物理环境层**
- **云与模型层**
- **数据与基础软件层**
- **应用与软件层**
- 以及一个常被忽视、但支撑全局的：**原材料与大宗商品层**

**核心理解：**  
上层业务越 “轻”，越依赖下层的物理投入。也就是说，**每一个 AI 应用背后，最终都离不开电力、材料、芯片、服务器和数据中心。**

---

##### 3. 原材料与大宗商品层：AI 热潮背后的 “隐形底座”
这一层常被忽略，但文章强调：**没有材料，就没有数据中心、芯片和电力网络扩张。**

###### 关键点
- **铜（Copper）**：最直接的 AI 大宗商品受益品种  
  - 用于：数据中心内外电缆、服务器 PCB 板、液冷系统、电网扩建  
  - 文章认为：AI、电气化、EV（电动车）共同推动**结构性铜牛市 / 超级周期**
  - 例子：**ERO、FCX、SCCO、COPX ETF**
- **铀与核燃料（Uranium）**  
  - 原因：大型云厂商（hyperscalers）开始签长期核电采购协议，以保障 AI 所需的稳定清洁基载电力
  - 例子：**CCJ、UEC、URA ETF**
- **稀土与特种金属**  
  - 用于：散热风扇、电机磁体、芯片制造和服务器组装中的钽、钨等
  - 例子：**MP**
- **天然气与能源基础设施**  
  - 在核能和可再生能源完全跟上之前，天然气仍是数据中心重要电源
  - 例子：**LNG、ET、WMB、XLE ETF**

**一句话理解：**  
AI 不只是 “软件革命”，也是一场**重资产、重能源、重材料**的工业建设潮。

---

##### 4. 硬件基础层：AI 最核心、最烧钱的一层
文章认为这是**最具战略意义、也最资本密集**的层级，因为所有 AI 模型训练和推理最终都靠这里。

###### 4.1 芯片制造设备
- 芯片不是凭空造出来的，必须依赖极高精度的设备
- 例子：**ASML 的 EUV 光刻机单台价格超过 3.5 亿美元**
- 相关公司：**ASML、AMAT、KLAC、CAMT**

###### 4.2 芯片设计软件与 IP
- 没有 EDA 工具和芯片架构授权，芯片根本设计不出来
- 这类公司虽然低调，但几乎出现在所有 GPU 和 ASIC 背后
- 相关公司：**ARM、SNPS、CDNS**

###### 4.3 晶圆代工
- 芯片最终要靠晶圆厂制造出来
- 文章强调：**先进制程晶圆厂建设成本可超过 200 亿美元**
- 因为门槛太高，全球玩家极少
- 相关公司：**TSMC、Samsung**

###### 4.4 定制芯片（ASIC）
- 为特定云计算工作负载量身定做
- 相比通用 GPU，往往有更好的**性能功耗比**
- 相关公司：**AVGO、MRVL**

###### 4.5 HBM 高带宽内存
- HBM 就像 AI 芯片的 “高速临时工作记忆”
- 如果带宽不足，GPU 会 “吃不饱数据”，性能就上不去
- 相关公司：**MU、SK Hynix、Samsung**

###### 4.6 先进封装
- 通过如**CoWoS**技术，把 HBM 堆叠到计算芯片旁边 / 上方
- 这样可以显著提高带宽、降低延迟
- 相关公司：**TSMC**

###### 4.7 服务器组装（ODM）
- 这一环负责把芯片、主板、电源、连接器、散热系统整合成可部署的 AI 服务器
- 例子：把 TSMC 生产的芯片装到像**NVIDIA HGX**这样的主板系统上
- 相关公司：**SMCI、Foxconn、Quanta**

###### 4.8 计算系统（GPU/TPU）
- 这是 AI 硬件平台的核心
- 文章特别指出，NVIDIA 的优势不只是 GPU 本身，还包括：
  - **GPU 计算能力**
  - **HBM 高速内存**
  - **NVLink 互联**
  - **CUDA 软件生态**
- 它真正做的是：把复杂硬件封装成开发者可直接使用的平台
- 重要数据：**NVIDIA 在 AI 训练芯片市场占有率约 80%+**
- 相关公司：**NVDA、GOOGL、AMD**

###### 4.9 网络互连
- 成千上万张 GPU 想像一台超级计算机一样协同工作，必须依靠超高速网络
- 如果网络太慢，GPU 之间协调效率下降，整体性能会明显变差
- 相关公司：**ANET、MRVL**

###### 4.10 边缘 AI 与垂直整合
- 有些公司自己设计芯片，但只给自己用，不对外卖
- 最典型例子是**苹果（Apple）**
  - 把**Neural Engine**集成进 M 系列和 A 系列芯片
  - 直接在设备端做 AI 推理，不必全部依赖云端
- 重要数据：**苹果拥有超过 20 亿活跃设备，是全球最大的边缘 AI 部署平台**
- 相关公司：**AAPL**

---

##### 5. 基础设施与物理环境层：让 AI 服务器真正跑起来
文章进一步指出：**AI 芯片非常耗电、发热极大**，单靠芯片本身远远不够，必须有匹配的物理环境。

###### 关键点
- **电力与散热**
  - 现代 AI 机柜热密度太高，传统风冷已不够，需要**液冷**
  - 电力系统也要为 AI 这种持续高负荷场景专门设计
  - 相关公司：**VRT、ETN、MOD**
- **数据中心地产**
  - AI 服务器需要专门的数据中心承载
  - 这类运营商提供建筑、电力接入、网络连接等基础条件

**一句话理解：**  
AI 数据中心的耗电量可能大到像 “一座小城市”，因此**电、冷却、机房**本身也是重要投资方向。

---

##### 6. 文章整体结论：AI 投资要看 “整条链”，而不是单点押注
- AI 的价值创造不是集中在单个明星公司，而是分布在**材料、能源、设备、设计、制造、封装、服务器、网络、云平台、模型和软件应用**等多个环节。
- **NVIDIA 当然重要，但它只是 AI 堆栈中的关键一层，不是全部。**
- 真正成熟的 AI 投资思路，是从 “谁在整个系统里持续受益” 出发，而不是只追逐最热门的名字。

---

- **单个 AI 数据中心耗电可接近一座小城市** → 说明电力、散热、地产不容忽视
