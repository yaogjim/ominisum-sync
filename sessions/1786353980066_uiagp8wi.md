## 正文

#### Everything You Do Is Being Recorded

Is there any way of fighting back?

![a photo of two friends talking at a bar, with a red waveform superimposed](https://cdn.theatlantic.com/thumbor/bKUCadzv8d55WC_3QHGart3HC94=/0x0:2000x1125/960x540/media/img/mt/2026/05/2026_05_15_Recording_arms_race_1/original.jpg)

Illustration by The Atlantic. Source: Getty.

Anthony “Bingy” Arillotta waited years to become a made man in the Genovese crime family, and when at last the call came in August 2003, he followed directions to the letter. According to sworn [testimony](https://www.villagevoice.com/to-get-in-the-mob-you-have-to-get-nude/), Arillotta was summoned to a steak house in the Bronx, where he was made to hand over his cellphone, beeper, and jewelry before being driven to an apartment building. When he got there, he was taken to a small bathroom and strip-searched for electronic devices. For his big meeting with the boss, he was given a bathrobe to wear.

Until recently, only spies and criminals had to worry this obsessively about their private statements being picked up by electronic equipment. But soon, the average person might need to deploy surveillance countermeasures. The next time you conduct a delicate bit of office diplomacy or share a romantic or financial secret with a friend over drinks, a sensor built into someone’s glasses, necklace, or lapel pin might be watching you and listening.

In March, the tech start-up Deveillance announced the development of Spectre I, a hockey-puck-shaped device that purports to prevent others from recording you (no strip search required). The company was founded by Aida Baradari, a recent college graduate who was worried by the surge in people wearing AI-enabled recorders. These wearables can be used as a silent notetaker, a personal assistant, or even a [therapist](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/10/23/whats-it-like-to-use-wearable-ai-tech) of sorts. That technology isn’t yet mainstream, but it may be soon. Apple—the company with the largest personal-tech ecosystem in the world—is rumored to be developing an AI pin or pendant that would serve as an iPhone’s constant eyes and ears; many other products of this type are on the way. AI accessories could one day be as widespread as AirPods.

#### 文章精炼总结

文章指出，随着具备录音、摄像和 AI 分析能力的可穿戴设备逐渐普及，普通人的日常言行可能随时被他人记录。过去主要属于间谍和罪犯的“反监控”需求，未来或将进入大众生活，并引发一场监控与反监控的“猫鼠游戏”。

##### 1. 从黑帮的极端防窃听说起

- 2003 年 8 月，Anthony “Bingy” Arillotta 在加入吉诺维斯犯罪家族时，经历了严格的电子设备排查。
- 根据宣誓证词，他先在纽约布朗克斯一家牛排馆交出手机、寻呼机和首饰，随后被带到一栋公寓楼。
- 在会见头目之前，他还在狭小的浴室里接受全身搜查，确认没有携带电子设备，最后只能穿着浴袍参加会面。
- 这一案例说明，过去只有间谍、犯罪组织等高风险群体，才会如此担心秘密谈话被电子设备捕捉。

##### 2. 普通人也可能需要防范无感记录

- 随着传感器越来越小，它们可以被安装在眼镜、项链、胸针等日常配饰中。
- 人们在进行职场沟通，或向朋友透露感情、财务等隐私时，可能在不知情的情况下被录音或录像。
- 因此，反监控措施未来可能不再是特殊职业的专属需求，而会成为普通人的隐私保护工具。

##### 3. 反录制设备开始出现

- 今年 3 月，科技初创公司 **Deveillance** 宣布开发名为 **Spectre I** 的设备。
- 该设备外形类似冰球，号称能够阻止他人录制使用者，无需像传统防窃听那样进行搜身。
- 公司创始人 **Aida Baradari** 是一名刚毕业不久的大学生。她创办公司的原因，是担忧越来越多人佩戴具备 AI 功能的录音设备。

##### 4. AI 可穿戴设备或将迅速普及

- AI 录音类穿戴设备可以充当无声的会议记录员、个人助理，甚至在一定程度上承担类似“心理陪伴或咨询”的角色。
- 这类产品目前尚未成为主流，但文章认为其普及可能很快到来。
- 据传，拥有庞大个人科技生态的 **苹果公司** 正在开发 AI 胸针或吊坠，使其成为 iPhone 持续运行的“眼睛和耳朵”。
- 其他公司也在推出类似产品。未来，AI 配饰可能像 **AirPods** 一样普遍，随之而来的隐私风险也将推动反录音、反监控技术发展。
