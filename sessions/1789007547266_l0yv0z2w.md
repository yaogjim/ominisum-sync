## 分析要点

- **核心高价值点**：开放协议只能保障技术可获得性，若关键材料供应脆弱且实验室自制成本过高，还必须建设与开放规范绑定的实体制造和交付体系。
- **留存建议**：作为科研基础设施产品化与供应链建设案例选择性保留（文章提供了从开放协议、关键材料补链到标准化试剂制造的完整案例，对深科技产品化和基础设施建设具有迁移价值。）

**知识要点**

- PURE是由36种蛋白质、31种小分子、43种tRNA混合物及核糖体组成的定义型转录翻译系统。
- 科研平台的可复现性不仅取决于协议开放，还受质粒完整性、材料许可、单一供应商和生产复杂度制约。
- 开放规范、可自行复现的协议与预制标准化产品可以并存，分别提供技术自主性和使用效率。

**行动项**

- 为关键技术栈建立组件清单，识别许可限制、单一供应商和停产风险。
- 将核心组件沉淀为开放规格、可验证协议和标准化物料，以提高互操作性。
- 比较用户自行制造与集中供应的时间、成本和可靠性，确定哪些基础组件适合产品化交付。

## 正文

*We started b.next as a few academics looking to solve the problem of interoperability and integration in synthetic cell research, and help our community realize the potential of synthetic cells. Over the last few years, we've been hard at work on this with Nucleus. But that alone isn't enough: synthetic cells can only go so far without a dedicated physical supply chain. We took on the challenge and now operate a manufacturing plant building Cytosol—the core materials needed to build cells—made to Nucleus open specifications. We recently shipped our 100th kit. Building industrial-grade infrastructure wasn’t necessarily what we expected would be needed when we set out, but it's a critical next step for synthetic cells to grow from an academic effort to solving global-scale problems. This post shares that journey.*

In 2023, we received a contract to build a simple synthetic cell sensor from fully open and specified components, as a means for jumpstarting collaborative and interoperable engineering of synthetic cells. A key part of our strategy was to build an open PURE system with easy-to-use protocols for construction, based on the [OnePot PURE system](https://pubs.acs.org/asbcd6/article-abstract/8/2/455/591614/A-Simple-Robust-and-Low-Cost-Method-To-Produce-the?redirectedFrom=fulltext). This was a clear need for the synthetic cell community, which had adopted the PURE system as its core platform, but practically only had access to non-extensible and non-modifiable commercial formulations. What we thought would take a few months turned into a multi-year undertaking as we uncovered foundational challenges in synthetic cell infrastructure and supply chains—and eventually led us to introduce and begin manufacturing “Cytosol” as a core substrate for building synthetic cells.

![](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69e805e3bdfff04b386ca6e9/6a9896ed2773c9a17674849a_cytosol-1.png)

PURE is a defined molecular system composed of 36 proteins, 31 small molecules, a mixture of 43 tRNA, and ribosomes that together power transcription and translation. Figure by Ganesh and Maerkl, 2024 used under CC-BY-4.0 with modifications.

##### This should be easy…right?

We were blocked at the very first step. We couldn’t acquire the 36 plasmids needed to implement the OnePot PURE system proteins. They were only available under restrictive material licensing terms (UBMTA), which as a company, we could not access—even though our goal was to make PURE better for the community. As we spoke with PURE researchers, we also learned that the available PURE plasmid set was incomplete and contained mutated plasmids. To our surprise, only a handful of labs around the world had actually successfully built the PURE system, often taking over 18 months to do so—a sobering length of time to get to a baseline technology first published in 2001.

We took this challenge head-on, re-designing and synthesizing our own improved, open-access set of PURE plasmids, and developing straightforward protocols for PURE protein production and assembly. After nine months of painstaking development, we could finally achieve detectable output—a critical milestone that confirmed that the open system could work at all. We shared our first set of [PURE protocols](https://docs.nucleus.engineering/docs/modules/base-cytosol/spec/) and [plasmids](https://docs.nucleus.engineering/docs/dna-distro-1/) in mid-2024 through the [Nucleus platform](https://nucleus.engineering/), which we had recently launched as an open commons for the community to build synthetic cells together.

![](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69e805e3bdfff04b386ca6e9/6a98a1841f309219b3390d4e_yemo-pure.jpg)

Yemo Ku—now b.next’s Manufacturing lead—popping champagne (”Ménage à 36”) to celebrate detectable output with our open PURE system in summer 2024. Nucleus v0.2 protocols for making PURE shown on the right.

##### We can't trust the supply chains.

But a functioning PURE system requires not just proteins—it also needs tRNA and ribosomes. When we started our work, tRNA were available from two providers: Roche and Sigma. In 2023, Roche stopped supplying tRNA. Then, just as we had gotten PURE proteins working with our open system, Sigma also stopped supplying tRNA. We learned that tRNA manufacturing had been deprioritized, and would be on back order indefinitely. A key reagent needed to build synthetic cells was suddenly unavailable.

We realized just how brittle the supply chains for synthetic cells were—a hodgepodge of different providers, often singular for a given reagent, none of whom cared about synthetic cell researchers in particular. As we began getting more requests for our open PURE DNA plasmid set, it became clear that achieving reliable synthetic cell engineering across the community would take more than we had originally planned for. The community needed reliable, open protocols not just for PURE proteins, but for every component.

![](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69e805e3bdfff04b386ca6e9/6a99e7c47995b45c9967bcc5_trna.jpg)

Our last precious tubes of tRNA, gifted by collaborators Kate Adamala and Evan Kalb.

##### The field needs more.

By mid-2025, we had developed and optimized open protocols, specifications, and materials for producing all PURE components—proteins, small molecules, tRNA, and ribosomes. We hosted our first [PURE workshop](https://bnext.bio/post/onepot-workshop-at-nucleus-labs)with 8 graduate students and post docs from major synthetic cell labs to begin disseminating the technology—an intensive sprint in which participants built the full PURE system from scratch in just one week. We did so with the support of Build A Cell and experts from the community, convening an earlier PURE Development Workshop to improve the open system and make it easy to use.

As we began working with more researchers, however, we became aware of a fundamental reality. No matter how accessible we made it, for any individual student or lab, making PURE was still a time-consuming process that only led to a limited amount of baseline reagent—just the starting point. Moreover, making any modifications or additions to the baseline PURE system usually led to manufacturing complexities that were not the expertise of individual labs. While validated, open PURE protocols ensured that any researcher has the ability to produce their own reagents if necessary, direct access to validated, ready-to-use reagents for building cells would simply be better, faster, and more reliable. We became convinced that to most effectively catalyze synthetic cell research, we needed to (1) start thinking in terms of “Cytosol”—PURE-like defined systems built to operate synthetic cells and be used by synthetic cell researchers, that could grow in functionality with supporting tooling alongside the field, and (2) provide pre-manufactured Cytosol kits tied to Nucleus open specifications and protocols.

![](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69e805e3bdfff04b386ca6e9/6a99e5c3d638ca759ee2417f_image.png)

We hosted a workshop in 2025 to teach synthetic cell researchers how to make PURE from fully open Nucleus components and processes.

##### A first synthetic cell manufacturing plant.

From late 2025 to early 2026 we built and operationalized a dedicated manufacturing plant in San Francisco to supply Cytosol as a foundation for synthetic cell engineering. In the last six months, we have supported open projects at \~20 labs around the world with over a hundred Cytosol kits, and are continuing to scale our capacities. Our Cytosol kits are built to [Nucleus open specifications](https://docs.nucleus.engineering/docs/modules/base-cytosol/spec/), and can be constructed from scratch by anyone via the openly available Nucleus protocols and plasmid set. We are actively testing and sharing new, open synthetic cell modules validated in and extending baseline Cytosol—including modules for [energy](https://devnotes.nucleus.engineering/articles/ppk-pure-cell-01), [control](https://devnotes.nucleus.engineering/articles/clpxp-module-plan), and [membrane translation](https://devnotes.nucleus.engineering/articles/yh-20260612-01). We have also been supporting collaborators with custom and modified Cytosol kits to enable more sophisticated synthetic cell development.

Going forward, we are excited to continue growing Cytosol’s capabilities alongside the community. We are starting to build digital and automation tools around Cytosol— for [design and analysis](https://pypi.org/project/nucleus-cdk/), high-throughput screening and optimization, as well as [AI-driven engineering](https://devnotes.nucleus.engineering/articles/d1-20260426) and module integration; and are helping facilitate multi-institutional research [programs](https://bnext.bio/programs)building open, integrated, synthetic cell technologies using Cytosol. Stable infrastructure and supply chains are foundational to achieving the potential of synthetic cells, and access to a common, open-formulation Cytosol is a subtle but important milestone for the community, one we’re happy to have reached. We look forward to continuing to advance these foundations with our manufacturing plant, with Nucleus, and with whatever else we discover is needed next. If you want to build with Cytosol, propose a module, or collaborate, please reach out—we are always looking for ways to build synthetic cells together.

![](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69e805e3bdfff04b386ca6e9/6a9b342aab8b9a20364cfa6a_bnext-cytosol-plant.jpg)

## 通俗精炼（1）

### 2026-09-10 02:31:04

#### 从开放协议到实体工厂：b.next 如何补上合成细胞的供应链

##### 一句话总结

b.next 原本只想建立一套开放、兼容的合成细胞研发标准，却发现关键材料长期受制于封闭授权和脆弱供应链，最终不得不自建生产工厂，将实验室技术变成可稳定交付的基础设施。

##### 起点：让合成细胞研发更开放

2023 年，b.next 开始尝试用完全开放、标准化的组件构建合成细胞传感器。

核心是复现并开放 **PURE 系统**——一套由蛋白质、tRNA、核糖体和小分子组成的体外转录翻译系统，相当于驱动合成细胞运行的基础“细胞质”。

但团队很快发现：

- 关键质粒受严格授权限制，企业无法获得；
- 现有质粒不完整，部分还存在突变；
- 全球仅少数实验室成功搭建过 PURE 系统；
- 从零构建通常需要超过 18 个月。

##### 第一步：重建开放版 PURE 系统

b.next 重新设计并合成了开放质粒，开发了更简单的蛋白生产和组装流程。

经过 9 个月研发，系统终于产生可检测输出。2024 年中，团队通过开放平台 **Nucleus** 发布了首批质粒与实验协议，让研究者可以自行复现。

##### 转折：技术开放还不够，供应链同样关键

PURE 系统还依赖 tRNA 和核糖体。当时全球只有少数供应商提供关键 tRNA：

- Roche 于 2023 年停止供应；
- Sigma 随后也停止供应；
- 关键试剂因此长期缺货。

这暴露出合成细胞领域的根本问题：供应商分散、关键材料常由单一厂商控制，而且传统厂商并不重视这一小众市场。

即使技术方案已经开放，没有稳定的材料供应，研究仍然无法规模化。

##### 从 PURE 到 Cytosol

到 2025 年中，b.next 已开发出 PURE 全部组件的开放生产方案，覆盖：

- 蛋白质；
- 小分子；
- tRNA；
- 核糖体。

团队还通过培训，让研究人员在一周内从零搭建完整系统。但实践证明，对单个实验室而言，自制试剂仍然耗时、产量有限，也难以支持复杂改造。

因此，b.next 提出了 **Cytosol**：一种面向合成细胞的标准化“细胞质底座”。它既可以按照开放协议自行生产，也可以直接购买经过验证的即用型试剂盒。

##### 最终结果：建成合成细胞材料工厂

2025 年末至 2026 年初，b.next 在旧金山建立了专门的生产工厂。截至文章发布时：

- 已向全球约 20 家实验室提供支持；
- 累计交付超过 100 套 Cytosol 试剂盒；
- 所有产品均遵循 Nucleus 的开放规格；
- 正在扩展能源、控制、膜蛋白翻译等功能模块；
- 可为合作方提供定制和改造版本。

团队还计划围绕 Cytosol 建设设计分析、自动化实验、高通量筛选及 AI 驱动的工程工具。

##### 核心启示

这段经历说明，前沿科研走向规模化不能只靠论文、软件和开放标准，还需要可靠的实体制造与供应链。

b.next 实际上构建了一套“**开放标准 + 可复现实验协议 + 标准化产品 + 专业制造 + 自动化工具**”的完整基础设施。其价值不只是销售试剂盒，而是降低合成细胞研究的启动成本，让不同实验室能够基于统一底座协作和创新。

## 结构化（1）

### 2026-09-10 04:19:07

#### 文章总结

b.next 原本只想解决合成细胞研究中的互操作与技术共享问题，却在推进开放版 PURE 系统时发现，真正的瓶颈是脆弱且封闭的实体供应链。为此，他们最终在旧金山建立了专门的制造工厂，生产符合开放规范的 **Cytosol（合成细胞的基础“细胞质”材料）**，推动合成细胞从学术研究走向规模化应用。

##### 1. 起点：为合成细胞建立开放、兼容的基础设施

- b.next 最初由几位学者创立，目标是解决合成细胞研究中组件难以兼容、成果难以复用的问题。
- 团队建立了开放协作平台 **Nucleus**，用于共享构建合成细胞所需的规范、质粒和实验流程。
- 2023 年，团队获得一份合同，要使用完全开放、定义明确的组件构建一个简单的合成细胞传感器。
- 其核心计划是基于 **OnePot PURE**，开发一套开放、易操作的 PURE 系统。

> PURE 是一种成分明确的无细胞转录翻译系统，由 **36 种蛋白质、31 种小分子、43 种 tRNA 的混合物以及核糖体**组成，可为基因转录和蛋白质翻译提供基础能力。

##### 2. 第一重障碍：连所需质粒都无法获得

- 团队最初以为几个月就能完成，但第一步就受阻：构建 OnePot PURE 所需的 **36 个质粒**受到严格的 UBMTA 材料许可限制，公司无法获取。
- 与研究人员交流后，他们进一步发现：
  - 现有 PURE 质粒集合并不完整；
  - 部分质粒已经发生突变；
  - 全球真正成功搭建 PURE 系统的实验室只有少数；
  - 一些实验室需要 **超过 18 个月**，才能复现这项早在 **2001 年**就已发表的基础技术。
- b.next 因此重新设计并合成了一套改良的开放质粒，同时开发更直接的蛋白生产与组装流程。
- 经过 **9 个月**反复调试，系统终于产生了可检测的输出。
- 2024 年年中，团队通过 Nucleus 发布了第一版开放 PURE 质粒和实验协议。

##### 3. 第二重障碍：关键试剂供应链突然中断

- PURE 不仅需要蛋白质，还需要 tRNA 和核糖体。
- 当时 tRNA 主要来自两家供应商：
  - **Roche** 于 2023 年停止供应；
  - 随后 **Sigma** 也停止供应，并表示相关生产已被降低优先级，产品将无限期缺货。
- 这意味着构建合成细胞所必需的关键试剂突然无法购买。
- 团队由此意识到，合成细胞供应链非常脆弱：
  - 不同组件来自分散的供应商；
  - 某些试剂只有唯一来源；
  - 供应商并不以合成细胞研究者为主要客户，随时可能停产。
- 因此，仅开放 PURE 蛋白质的生产方法还不够，必须为**所有核心组件**建立可验证、开放且可持续的生产方案。

##### 4. 从开放协议到可直接使用的 Cytosol

- 到 **2025 年年中**，b.next 已为 PURE 的全部组件建立并优化了开放协议、规格和材料，包括：
  - 蛋白质；
  - 小分子；
  - tRNA；
  - 核糖体。
- 团队还举办了首场 PURE 培训工作坊，邀请来自主要合成细胞实验室的 **8 名研究生和博士后**参加。
- 在 Build A Cell 和社区专家支持下，参与者仅用 **1 周**就从零构建出完整 PURE 系统，证明开放流程具备可传播性和可复现性。

但团队也发现，开放协议仍不能彻底解决问题：

- 对单个学生或实验室而言，自制 PURE 依然费时费力；
- 最终只能得到数量有限的基础试剂；
- 一旦要修改或扩展系统，就会遇到超出普通实验室专长的制造难题。

因此，b.next 的思路发生了转变：

1. 不再只把 PURE 看作转录翻译系统，而是将其发展为可驱动合成细胞的通用基础材料 **Cytosol**；
2. 除了公开制作方法，还应直接提供经过验证、开箱即用的 Cytosol 套件；
3. 所有套件均与 Nucleus 的开放规范、质粒和协议对应，研究者仍可自行复现和修改。

##### 5. “意外”建成合成细胞制造工厂

- 从 **2025 年末到 2026 年初**，b.next 在旧金山建设并投入运营了一座专门的制造工厂，用于规模化生产 Cytosol。
- 在最近 **6 个月**中，团队已向全球约 **20 家实验室**的开放研究项目提供了 **超过 100 套 Cytosol**，并刚刚完成第 100 套产品的交付。
- 这些套件具有两项关键特点：
  - 可直接用于合成细胞研究；
  - 完全按照 Nucleus 的开放规范制造，任何人都可以利用公开协议和质粒从头复现。
- 团队还在开发和验证能够扩展基础 Cytosol 的开放模块，包括：
  - 能量模块；
  - 控制模块；
  - 膜蛋白翻译模块。
- 对于更复杂的项目，b.next 也会为合作方提供定制或改造后的 Cytosol 套件。

##### 6. 下一步：自动化、AI 与跨机构协作

b.next 计划围绕 Cytosol 继续建设配套工具和研究生态，包括：

- 用于设计和分析的数字化工具；
- 高通量筛选与优化系统；
- AI 驱动的工程设计和模块整合；
- 支持多机构共同开发开放、集成式合成细胞技术。

##### 核心结论

这段经历表明，合成细胞技术要从少数实验室的探索走向大规模科研和现实应用，仅有论文、软件平台或开放协议并不足够，还必须具备**稳定的制造能力、可靠的供应链和统一开放的基础材料**。b.next 建立 Cytosol 工厂并非最初计划，而是解决现实基础设施缺口后必然迈出的一步。
